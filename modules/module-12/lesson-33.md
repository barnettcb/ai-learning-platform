# Lesson 33 — What Should an AI Project Handoff Contain?

## Outcome
You can create a concise handoff that allows a person or a new AI session to continue work without relearning the entire history.

## Direct answer
A handoff should preserve current truth, important reasoning, and the exact continuation point. It should not reproduce every message.

Include:
- **Purpose:** what the project is trying to accomplish;
- **Current status:** what is complete, in progress, blocked, or unstarted;
- **Approved decisions:** choices that still govern the work;
- **Key evidence:** facts, files, and sources that matter;
- **Constraints:** limits, exclusions, and quality requirements;
- **Open issues:** unresolved questions or risks;
- **Rejected paths:** only those likely to be reconsidered accidentally;
- **Next action:** the specific task to perform next;
- **Verification state:** what has and has not been checked.

## Prefer current truth over chronology
A chronological transcript explains how the project unfolded but forces the next worker to reconstruct the present state.

A good handoff starts with the present. History appears only when it explains a decision, warning, or unresolved conflict.

## Make status visible
Use unambiguous labels such as:
- complete;
- approved;
- draft;
- needs verification;
- blocked;
- superseded;
- next.

Avoid phrases like “we discussed” when the handoff should state what was actually decided.

## Worked example
A travel-planning handoff records the approved dates, budget, accessibility needs, refundable-booking preference, verified flight options, and the next comparison to perform. It does not include every destination brainstorm that was rejected.

## Best practice
Write the handoff so a careful newcomer can identify the next step and the rules governing it within two minutes.

## Common failure
The handoff is exhaustive but not usable. Important decisions are buried inside narrative, and superseded information appears beside current instructions without labels.

## Guided practice
Condense a fictional ten-message project history into a handoff with current status, decisions, evidence, risks, and next action.

## Independent application
Create a handoff for a real or simulated ongoing project. Give it to another person or reread it after a break, then revise any part that leaves the continuation point unclear.

## Completion check
You are ready to continue when the handoff communicates what is true now, why it matters, and what happens next.
