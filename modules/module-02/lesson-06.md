# Lesson 6 — What Information Is Safe to Share?

## Outcome
You can reduce privacy risk by deciding what information is necessary before entering text, files, or images into an AI system.

## Direct answer
Share the minimum information needed for the task. Remove or replace details that identify a person, expose confidential material, reveal credentials, or create unnecessary risk.

Before sharing information, ask:

1. Does the AI need this detail to help?
2. Could I remove, summarize, or replace it?
3. Am I allowed to share it?
4. Would I be comfortable if it were retained, reviewed, or exposed?

If any answer creates concern, stop and reduce the information or use an approved alternative.

## Information that deserves special caution
Do not casually enter:
- passwords, security codes, private keys, or recovery information;
- full financial account or payment-card details;
- government identification numbers;
- private medical records with identifying details;
- confidential workplace, customer, legal, or contract information;
- information about another person that you do not have permission to share;
- private images or documents containing hidden identifying details;
- precise personal details that are unnecessary for the task.

## Minimize before you share
Useful techniques include:
- replace names with roles such as “Customer A” or “Employee 1”;
- remove account numbers, addresses, signatures, and identifiers;
- summarize the relevant section instead of uploading the full file;
- provide approximate numbers when exact values are unnecessary;
- crop images to the area that matters;
- use an employer-approved system for workplace material;
- describe the structure of a problem without exposing the underlying confidential data.

## Worked example
### Original request
“Review this employee complaint and tell me how to respond.”

The attached document includes names, medical details, phone numbers, and internal personnel notes.

### Safer approach
Remove names and contact details. Exclude medical information that is not needed. Summarize the relevant events, label people by role, and use only an approved workplace tool. Human resources or legal review may still be required.

The task may remain useful for AI assistance, but the full original file is not necessary.

## Best practice
Do not ask whether information is merely interesting or available. Ask whether it is necessary.

## Common failure
People remove a person's name but leave enough details to identify them, such as employer, job title, location, exact dates, and a unique incident. Privacy protection requires looking at the whole combination of details.

## Guided practice
How could each item be made safer?

1. A bank statement used to categorize spending.
2. A medical message used to prepare appointment questions.
3. A customer complaint used to improve a response.
4. A photograph used to identify a household part.

Possible safeguards:
1. Remove names, account numbers, addresses, and unrelated transactions; use an approved tool.
2. Remove direct identifiers and include only the information needed to prepare questions.
3. Replace names and order details; remove contact and payment information.
4. Crop the image and check for faces, addresses, labels, reflections, or location information.

## Independent application
Choose one file, message, or image you might use with AI. List:
- what the system actually needs;
- what can be removed;
- what should be replaced with a neutral label;
- whether the tool is approved for that information.

## Completion check
You are ready to continue when you can reduce an input to the minimum useful information and recognize when permission or an approved system is required.
