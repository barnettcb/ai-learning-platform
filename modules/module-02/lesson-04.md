# Lesson 4 — When Is AI Useful?

## Outcome
You can recognize tasks where AI is likely to save time, improve thinking, or create a useful starting point.

## Direct answer
AI is most useful when it can help you produce, organize, transform, or explore information without becoming the final authority.

A task is usually a good fit when:
- the goal can be stated clearly;
- useful information can be provided or gathered;
- the result can be reviewed;
- mistakes can be corrected;
- the learner remains responsible for important decisions.

Common useful roles include:
- **explainer:** clarify an unfamiliar idea;
- **drafter:** create a first version;
- **editor:** improve existing material;
- **organizer:** turn scattered information into a structure;
- **brainstorming partner:** generate possibilities;
- **question generator:** identify gaps or issues to investigate;
- **practice partner:** create examples, quizzes, or simulations;
- **analysis assistant:** compare information using criteria supplied by the learner.

## A practical test
Ask three questions:

1. Can I describe what help I need?
2. Can I review the result or verify what matters?
3. Will a mistake be manageable?

If the answer to all three is yes, AI is often a reasonable tool for at least part of the task.

## Worked example
### Situation
You need to prepare for a difficult meeting.

### Useful AI roles
- organize the facts you provide;
- identify likely questions;
- suggest ways to explain the issue clearly;
- help draft an agenda;
- simulate possible responses.

### Limits
The AI does not know the people, history, workplace culture, or consequences unless you explain them. It also cannot take responsibility for the meeting.

The task is a good fit because the output is preparatory, reviewable, and adjustable.

## Best practice
Use AI where it creates leverage: a faster start, a clearer structure, more options, or a better review.

## Common failure
People sometimes use AI simply because it is available. This can add work instead of reducing it. A simple task that already takes two minutes may not need an AI workflow.

## Guided practice
For each task, name the most useful AI role.

1. Turn rough notes into a meeting agenda.
2. Learn the difference between two unfamiliar terms.
3. Generate ideas for reorganizing a closet.
4. Review a draft for unclear wording.
5. Create practice questions from study notes.

Possible answers:
1. Organizer
2. Explainer
3. Brainstorming partner
4. Editor
5. Practice partner

## Independent application
Choose one recurring task from work or home. Write:
- what part takes the most effort;
- which AI role could help;
- what you would still need to review or decide.

## Completion check
You are ready to continue when you can identify a specific useful role for AI instead of treating the whole task as something to hand over.
