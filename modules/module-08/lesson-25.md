# Lesson 25 — How Can AI Help With Hobbies, Travel, and Everyday Life?

## Outcome
You can use AI for ordinary activities by turning personal preferences and practical limits into a useful plan without treating generic suggestions as personalized certainty.

## Direct answer
Describe the real-life situation, preferences, and constraints that determine whether a suggestion is useful.

Everyday applications may include:
- planning meals or outings;
- exploring a hobby;
- organizing a household task;
- preparing for a purchase;
- comparing travel options;
- generating practice ideas;
- troubleshooting a noncritical problem;
- creating routines or checklists.

## Replace generic categories with usable details
A request becomes more personal when it includes factors such as:
- available time;
- budget;
- location;
- experience level;
- equipment already owned;
- mobility or accessibility needs;
- preferred pace;
- dislikes;
- companions, pets, or children;
- tolerance for crowds, weather, risk, or complexity.

## Worked example
### Generic request
“Plan a weekend trip.”

### Useful request
“Plan a two-night driving trip within three hours of Houston for two adults and a dog. Prefer quiet outdoor activities, uncrowded restaurants with patios, and a hotel with easy parking. Keep lodging under $220 per night and include a bad-weather alternative.”

The second request provides the conditions that shape a realistic plan.

## Use AI to prepare, not to guarantee
For plans involving current details, verify:
- opening hours;
- prices;
- reservations;
- pet or age restrictions;
- weather;
- road conditions;
- cancellation rules;
- product availability.

For troubleshooting, distinguish harmless experiments from situations requiring a manufacturer, tradesperson, clinician, or other qualified professional.

## Ask for tradeoffs
A useful recommendation should explain why an option fits and what compromise it requires.

Example:

“Give me three options: easiest, lowest cost, and best overall fit. Explain the tradeoff in each and identify the facts I should confirm.”

## Best practice
Provide the few preferences that actually change the answer. More personal detail is not automatically better, especially when it is sensitive or irrelevant.

## Common failure
Learners ask for “the best” option without defining best. The AI fills the gap with common assumptions that may not match the user's priorities.

## Guided practice
Improve this request:

“Help me start gardening.”

One possible revision:

“I have a small south-facing patio, four hours of direct sun, six containers, and about two hours per week for care. Suggest a beginner garden for a hot climate, explain what may struggle in those conditions, and give me a first-month checklist.”

## Independent application
Choose an everyday decision or hobby task. Identify the five constraints or preferences most likely to change the answer, then request options with visible tradeoffs and confirmation needs.

## Completion check
You are ready to continue when recommendations are judged against your stated priorities rather than how polished or popular they sound.
