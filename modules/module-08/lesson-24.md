# Lesson 24 — How Can AI Help Me Learn a Subject or Skill?

## Outcome
You can use AI as an adaptive tutor by defining a learning goal, testing understanding, and practicing rather than only reading explanations.

## Direct answer
Ask AI to create a learning process, not merely provide information.

A useful tutoring workflow includes:
1. Define the skill or knowledge goal.
2. Establish the learner's starting point.
3. Break the subject into a sensible sequence.
4. Explain one concept at a time.
5. Require retrieval, practice, or application.
6. Correct misunderstandings.
7. Reassess progress and adjust difficulty.

## State the learning target
“Teach me photography” is too broad.

A clearer target is:

“Teach me enough manual camera control to photograph a moving dog outdoors. Assume I understand basic camera menus but do not understand shutter speed, aperture, or ISO.”

The target defines both the outcome and the starting level.

## Make the learner do the work
Understanding feels stronger than it is when the AI supplies every answer.

Ask for:
- one question at a time;
- short recall checks;
- practice problems;
- feedback on your explanation;
- examples followed by a new case;
- increasingly difficult applications;
- correction without immediately revealing the full answer.

## Worked example
### Passive request
“Explain compound interest.”

### Active learning request
“Explain compound interest in plain language with one example. Then ask me to explain it back in my own words. Correct any misunderstanding and give me one practice problem without showing the answer until I respond.”

The second request turns explanation into learning.

## Handle uncertainty
AI can teach an error convincingly. For factual, technical, or high-stakes subjects, combine tutoring with suitable references and verification.

Also tell the AI when:
- a course, manual, or textbook must be followed;
- terminology should match a known standard;
- the answer should remain within supplied material;
- expert instruction is necessary for physical safety.

## Best practice
Measure progress through performance. The learner should solve, explain, create, or demonstrate—not merely report that the explanation made sense.

## Common failure
Learners keep requesting simpler explanations but never identify the exact point of confusion or attempt the task themselves.

## Guided practice
Turn this into a tutoring request:

“I want to learn Excel.”

One possible revision:

“I want to learn how to clean and summarize a monthly expense list in Excel. Assess what I know with three questions, create a five-step learning sequence, and give me a small practice task after each step.”

## Independent application
Choose a skill you genuinely want to improve. Define the target performance, current level, practice method, feedback method, and evidence that would show progress.

## Completion check
You are ready to continue when your learning interaction requires you to retrieve or apply knowledge rather than only consume explanations.
