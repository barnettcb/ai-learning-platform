# Lesson 23 — How Should I Use AI for Medical Information and Appointment Preparation?

## Outcome
You can use AI to understand medical language and prepare for care without relying on it to diagnose, dismiss urgent symptoms, or replace a clinician.

## Direct answer
Use AI as an educational and organizational assistant. Do not use it as the final authority for diagnosis, treatment, medication changes, or emergency decisions.

Appropriate supporting roles include:
- translating medical terminology into plain language;
- organizing a symptom timeline;
- summarizing questions for an appointment;
- explaining the purpose of common tests;
- comparing general categories of treatment;
- reviewing a clinician-provided plan for points that need clarification;
- preparing a concise handoff of records and prior events.

## Put safety first
Seek appropriate medical or emergency care instead of continuing an AI conversation when symptoms may be urgent, severe, rapidly worsening, or otherwise concerning.

AI lacks physical examination, complete records, reliable situational awareness, and clinical responsibility. Even a calm explanation can be wrong for the person asking.

## Provide structure, not a self-diagnosis
For appointment preparation, organize:
- the main concern;
- onset and timeline;
- pattern, severity, and triggers;
- associated symptoms;
- relevant diagnoses and procedures;
- current medications and recent changes;
- measurements or test results with dates;
- questions and decisions needed from the clinician.

Do not force the information into a preferred diagnosis.

## Worked example
### Weak request
“Is this chest tightness just anxiety?”

### Better request
“Help me organize a concise symptom summary for a clinician. Separate what I observed from possible explanations. Flag details that may affect urgency, and do not conclude that the symptom is anxiety or rule out a medical cause.”

The improved request supports communication without pretending to settle the diagnosis.

## Verify medical information
When learning about a condition or treatment:
- favor current, authoritative health sources;
- distinguish general information from personal applicability;
- confirm medication and interaction questions with a pharmacist or prescriber;
- note when guidelines differ by country, age, diagnosis, or risk;
- bring unresolved questions to the treating professional.

## Best practice
Ask AI to produce an appointment-ready question list ordered by importance, with the observations supporting each question.

## Common failure
The learner describes only selected symptoms, receives a plausible explanation, and uses it to delay care or to change treatment without professional guidance.

## Guided practice
Improve this request:

“Explain my test result.”

One possible revision:

“Explain the terms in this test result using only the report I provide. Separate the report's findings from general background information, identify what cannot be concluded from the report alone, and help me prepare questions for the ordering clinician.”

## Independent application
Create a brief, privacy-conscious appointment preparation note from a real or fictional scenario. Include observations, dated facts, medication information, and questions without adding a diagnosis.

## Completion check
You are ready to continue when you can use AI to improve communication with a clinician without allowing it to replace clinical evaluation.
