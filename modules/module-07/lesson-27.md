# Lesson 27 — How Should I Work With Images?

## Outcome
You can use AI to describe, inspect, compare, or transform images while accounting for ambiguity and visual-reading errors.

## Direct answer
Define the visual task precisely and separate visible observations from interpretation.

AI can often help with:
- describing a scene;
- reading visible text;
- identifying objects or layout elements;
- comparing images;
- explaining a chart or diagram;
- suggesting design improvements;
- generating or editing visual content.

It may misread small text, overlook details, confuse similar objects, infer context that is not visible, or describe unclear areas too confidently.

## Choose the task type
A visual request should make clear whether you want:
- **description:** what is visibly present;
- **extraction:** text, labels, values, or attributes;
- **comparison:** meaningful differences between images;
- **interpretation:** what the visual may indicate;
- **transformation:** a requested edit or newly generated image.

These tasks require different review standards.

## Control inference
For inspection tasks, use language such as:

“First describe only what is clearly visible. Then provide a separate section for possible interpretations. Mark anything uncertain or unreadable.”

This is especially important for screenshots, damage photos, medical images, charts, and unfamiliar equipment.

## Provide useful visual context
Mention:
- what the image is supposed to show;
- where the important area is located;
- the decision or question it supports;
- whether exact text or measurements matter;
- whether there are multiple images in a sequence.

## Worked example
### Weak request
“What is wrong here?”

### Better request
“This is a screenshot from a thermostat that has stopped responding to touch. Describe the visible display state, list any error indicators or unusual screen behavior, and distinguish what the image confirms from possible causes that would require testing.”

The revision prevents diagnosis from being blended with observation.

## Best practice
Zoom, crop, or provide multiple angles when the important detail is small, obstructed, or dependent on perspective.

## Common failure
Learners assume an image contains enough information to establish cause. A photo may document appearance without proving timing, function, internal condition, or surrounding context.

## Guided practice
Rewrite this chart request:

“Explain this graph.”

One possible revision:

“Identify the chart title, axes, units, time period, major trend, largest change, and any visible annotations. Then explain two reasonable interpretations and any limitation that prevents a firm conclusion.”

## Independent application
Choose an image and write a request that defines the task type, important region, required details, inference boundary, and uncertainty handling.

## Completion check
You are ready to continue when you can distinguish what an image shows from what someone believes caused it.
