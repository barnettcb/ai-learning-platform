# Lesson 20 — How Should I Use AI for Planning and Project Management?

## Outcome
You can use AI to turn a defined objective into an organized, reviewable plan while keeping dates, ownership, dependencies, and feasibility grounded in reality.

## Direct answer
Use AI to structure the planning work, not to invent certainty.

A useful planning sequence is:
1. define the result;
2. list known requirements and constraints;
3. divide the work into meaningful parts;
4. identify dependencies and decision points;
5. assign owners and target dates;
6. expose assumptions, risks, and missing information;
7. review the plan with the people responsible for delivering it.

## Start with planning facts
Before requesting a plan, provide what is actually known:
- the intended outcome;
- the deadline or time window;
- people and resources available;
- fixed requirements;
- work already completed;
- known dependencies;
- approval points;
- risks or uncertainties.

When information is missing, instruct AI to mark the gap instead of filling it silently.

## Worked example
### Weak request
“Create a launch plan for my website.”

### Better request
“Create a first-pass launch plan for a five-page informational website that must be live by October 15. Content drafting, design, development, review, and launch testing are required. One person owns content and one contractor owns development. Identify dependencies, decisions that must be made, and assumptions that need confirmation. Do not assign exact dates where the available information is insufficient.”

The second request produces a planning draft that can be challenged and corrected.

## Review the plan as a model
A polished schedule can still be unrealistic. Check whether:
- each task contributes to the stated result;
- dependencies are in the correct order;
- responsibilities are explicit;
- durations are plausible;
- review and correction time exists;
- external approvals are represented;
- hidden assumptions have been exposed;
- risks have responses rather than labels alone.

## Best practice
Ask AI to create the plan and then critique it from the perspective of execution: “What could prevent this plan from working as written?”

## Common failure
The learner accepts a detailed plan because it looks complete. The plan contains invented durations, unavailable resources, or tasks arranged in a convenient rather than feasible order.

## Guided practice
Improve this request:

“Plan a family reunion.”

One possible revision:

“Create a planning checklist for a family reunion for approximately 35 people in early June. Separate decisions, reservations, communications, supplies, and day-of tasks. Show dependencies and recommended decision deadlines, but label any date that depends on the venue or attendee responses.”

## Independent application
Choose a real project. Ask AI for a first-pass plan, identify at least three assumptions, and revise the plan using information from the people or sources that control those assumptions.

## Completion check
You are ready to continue when you can distinguish an organized planning draft from a confirmed execution plan.
