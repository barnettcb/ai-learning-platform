# Lesson 17 — How Do I Verify an AI-Supported Answer?

## Outcome
You can identify claims that need verification and judge whether a source is suitable for the claim being made.

## Direct answer
Verify claims according to their importance, changeability, and source requirements.

Not every sentence needs the same scrutiny. Verification becomes more important when a claim is:
- high stakes;
- current or likely to change;
- numerical;
- surprising;
- disputed;
- specific to a law, policy, product, or person;
- central to a decision;
- outside your own knowledge.

## Match the source to the claim
Prefer sources closest to the underlying information.

Examples:
- law or regulation → official government source;
- product capability → current manufacturer documentation;
- medical guidance → recognized clinical or public-health authority;
- research finding → the original study or authoritative review;
- company financial result → official filing or investor report;
- event details → primary announcement plus reputable independent reporting when needed.

A source can be credible in general and still be wrong for a specific claim.

## Check more than the citation
A citation does not automatically prove the sentence.

Confirm:
1. The source actually exists.
2. The source says what the answer claims.
3. The date is appropriate.
4. The context has not been distorted.
5. The source is authoritative enough for the use.
6. Other reliable sources do not materially contradict it.

## Worked example
Claim: “This tax credit is available to all homeowners.”

Verification questions:
- Which jurisdiction?
- Which tax year?
- What eligibility rules apply?
- Is the information from the responsible tax authority?
- Does “available” mean refundable, nonrefundable, or merely claimable under conditions?

The original sentence may be too broad even if a credit exists.

## Best practice
Verify the claims that carry the decision. Do not waste equal effort checking harmless background details while leaving the central number or rule untested.

## Common failure
Learners ask AI, “Are you sure?” The AI may repeat the same claim more confidently.

Verification requires checking evidence, not requesting reassurance.

## Guided practice
Rank these claims by verification priority:
- A recipe serves four.
- A medication can be safely combined with another medication.
- A museum opens at 10 a.m. tomorrow.
- A historical event occurred in 1776.

The medication claim is high stakes. The museum time is current and changeable. The historical date is stable but should still be checked if central to formal work. The recipe serving estimate is usually low stakes.

## Independent application
Take a recent AI answer and mark its three most decision-relevant claims. For each, identify the best source type and what you must confirm.

## Completion check
You are ready to continue when you can explain why a source is appropriate for a particular claim rather than merely calling it trustworthy.
