# Lesson 16 — How Should I Use AI for Research?

## Outcome
You can use AI to frame a research question, organize findings, and identify gaps without treating its first answer as the evidence.

## Direct answer
Use AI as a research assistant, not as the final authority.

AI is useful for:
- narrowing a broad topic;
- identifying subquestions;
- suggesting search terms;
- explaining unfamiliar concepts;
- organizing supplied evidence;
- comparing positions;
- identifying missing information;
- drafting a synthesis after sources are reviewed.

## Begin with a research question
A topic is not yet a research assignment.

Topic:
“Home solar panels”

Research question:
“What financial, roof, utility, and ownership factors should a homeowner evaluate before requesting solar installation quotes?”

The question defines what information belongs in the research.

## Separate discovery from conclusion
A reliable sequence is:
1. Define the question and scope.
2. Identify the information needed.
3. Locate appropriate sources.
4. Review the evidence.
5. Compare agreements, conflicts, and gaps.
6. Draft the conclusion with uncertainty visible.

AI can assist at each stage, but the source material—not the fluency of the response—supports the conclusion.

## Worked example
### Weak request
“Research electric vehicles and tell me whether I should buy one.”

### Better research assignment
“Help me research whether an electric vehicle fits a household that drives 12,000 miles per year, parks in a garage, and takes four long road trips annually. First identify the decision factors and information I need. Do not recommend a vehicle yet.”

The revised request turns an oversized conclusion into a research plan.

## Best practice
Ask AI to expose the research structure before asking it to synthesize an answer.

## Common failure
Learners request a polished report before defining the question or collecting evidence. The resulting report may be coherent but built on unstated assumptions and incomplete information.

## Guided practice
Turn this topic into a research question:

“Starting a vegetable garden”

Possible answer:

“What sunlight, soil, climate, space, cost, and maintenance factors should a beginner evaluate before choosing vegetables for a first home garden?”

## Independent application
Choose a real topic. Write one research question, define what is outside scope, and ask AI to propose the evidence categories needed before reaching a conclusion.

## Completion check
You are ready to continue when the research process can reveal that the available evidence is insufficient rather than forcing an answer.
