# Capstone — Complete a Real AI-Assisted Project

## Purpose
Demonstrate that you can use AI as part of a complete, responsible workflow rather than merely produce a strong individual prompt.

## Choose the project
Select a meaningful task that:
- requires more than one step;
- uses real constraints or source material;
- benefits from iteration;
- includes at least one factual or quality check;
- produces a usable final deliverable;
- remains safe to complete as a learning project.

Possible projects include a trip plan, research brief, household decision, learning plan, event proposal, document package, hobby project, or non-sensitive work plan.

## Required phases
### 1. Decide
Explain why AI is appropriate, what role it will play, what should remain human-controlled, and what information should not be shared.

### 2. Define
State the outcome, audience, boundaries, success criteria, and assumptions requiring confirmation.

### 3. Gather
Identify source files, current web information, examples, and missing evidence. Distinguish supplied facts from AI-generated suggestions.

### 4. Collaborate
Complete the work in suitable stages. Record important revisions and decisions rather than preserving every conversational turn.

### 5. Verify
Check task fit, evidence, reasoning, completeness, usability, risk, and ownership. Use authoritative sources or professional review where the project requires them.

### 6. Deliver
Produce the final usable result in the format needed by its intended audience.

### 7. Preserve
Create a concise project record, reusable assets, and a handoff or closeout note.

## Keep a concise project record
Preserve only what will help you explain, review, or reuse the work:
1. the project brief, AI role, and boundaries;
2. the evidence, constraints, and important assumptions;
3. the key prompts, revisions, and consequential decisions;
4. the final deliverable;
5. the verification and review record;
6. a short reflection and handoff or closeout note.

## Evaluation standard
The project is successful when the process is understandable, the result is useful, important claims are checked, risks are controlled, and the learner can explain every consequential decision without attributing responsibility to AI.
