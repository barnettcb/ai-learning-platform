What Can AI Help Me Do?

What you will be able to do

You can recognize the kinds of work conversational AI usually handles well and the kinds of work that still require strong human control.

The key idea

Conversational AI is most useful when the task involves language, patterns, organization, explanation, or generating possibilities. It is less dependable when the task requires guaranteed facts, private judgment, real-world observation, or accountability for a decision.

AI can often help you:

  • explain unfamiliar material;
  • brainstorm possibilities;
  • draft or revise writing;
  • summarize information you provide;
  • organize a plan;
  • compare options using criteria you define;
  • identify questions you may have overlooked;
  • transform information from one format into another.

AI should not be treated as:

  • an automatic source of truth;
  • a substitute for direct inspection of the real world;
  • the final decision-maker in consequential situations;
  • a person who understands your circumstances without being told;
  • an accountable professional.

A useful distinction

The best question is usually not, “Can AI do this?” It is:

Which part of this task can AI help with, and which part must remain under human control?

For example, AI can help prepare questions for a medical appointment. It cannot examine you, confirm a diagnosis, or accept responsibility for treatment. AI can help compare two financial strategies using assumptions you provide. It cannot know that those assumptions are correct or decide how much risk your household should accept.

Example

Task

Plan a weekend trip.

AI can help with

  • generating destination ideas;
  • organizing an itinerary;
  • creating a packing list;
  • comparing options using stated preferences.

Human responsibility

  • confirm current prices, hours, restrictions, and availability;
  • decide whether the plan fits the travelers;
  • make reservations;
  • respond to real-world changes.

The whole task is not simply “an AI task” or “not an AI task.” It contains several smaller tasks with different levels of suitability.

Best practice Use AI as an assistant for thinking and producing work, not as an unquestioned authority.

Watch for this A learner asks AI for a complete answer, receives polished language, and assumes the task is finished. The missing step is deciding what still requires review, verification, or personal judgment.

Try it

For each task, identify one helpful AI role and one human responsibility.

  1. Write a polite complaint to a service provider.
  2. Decide whether a new symptom requires emergency care.
  3. Create ideas for a birthday celebration.
  4. Determine the exact amount owed on a tax return.

Suggested evaluation

  1. AI can draft; the person confirms facts and sends the message.
  2. AI may help organize information, but urgent medical triage must follow reliable professional or emergency guidance.
  3. AI can generate options; the person chooses based on the guest of honor and real constraints.
  4. AI may explain concepts or help review inputs, but the calculation and filing require verified rules, records, and accountable review.

Apply it

Choose one task you expect to use AI for this month. Divide it into:

  • work AI can assist with;
  • facts or inputs you must supply;
  • decisions you must make;
  • details you must verify.

Check yourself

You are ready to continue when you can explain why a task may be partly suitable for AI without being safe to delegate completely.

Pause and decide

Which use keeps the right human role?
