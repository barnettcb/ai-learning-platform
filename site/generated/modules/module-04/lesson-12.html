How Do I Keep Assumptions and Uncertainty Visible?

What you will be able to do

You can ask AI to identify what it is assuming, what it does not know, and which conclusions need confirmation.

The key idea

When missing information could change the result, require AI to separate known facts, assumptions, uncertainties, and questions.

AI often fills gaps smoothly. That can make an incomplete answer appear more settled than it is.

Four useful labels

Ask the response to distinguish:

  • Known: supplied facts or well-supported information;
  • Assumed: details treated as true without confirmation;
  • Uncertain: information that may be incomplete, variable, or disputed;
  • Need to ask: missing facts that should be obtained before proceeding.

Example

Request

“Estimate whether this home project can be finished in one weekend.”

The answer depends on project size, tools, experience, drying time, help available, and hidden damage.

A stronger instruction is:

“Before estimating, list the facts you know, the assumptions you would otherwise make, and the questions whose answers could materially change the estimate.”

This does not eliminate uncertainty. It makes uncertainty manageable.

Use confidence carefully

A confidence label can be useful, but a number such as “85% confident” may look more precise than the evidence allows.

Prefer explanations such as:

  • high confidence because the conclusion follows directly from supplied facts;
  • moderate confidence because two important details are missing;
  • low confidence because the result depends on current or specialized information.

Best practice Ask for visible assumptions before relying on recommendations, estimates, interpretations, or plans built from incomplete information.

Watch for this Learners ask AI to “be accurate” or “do not make assumptions.” Those instructions sound strong but do not create a process for handling missing information.

A better instruction is:

“Do not silently fill important gaps. Identify them and ask for the information needed.”

Try it

Improve this request:

“Tell me why sales dropped.”

One possible revision:

“Based only on the information provided, separate observed facts from possible explanations. List the additional data needed to test each explanation, and do not present a cause as established unless the evidence supports it.”

Apply it

Add an assumption-and-uncertainty instruction to the staged task from Lesson 11. Limit it to the stage where uncertainty matters.

Check yourself

You are ready to continue when the response structure makes it difficult to mistake an assumption for a fact.

Pause and decide

How should uncertainty appear in a useful answer?
