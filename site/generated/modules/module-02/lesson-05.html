When Should I Not Use AI?

What you will be able to do

You can identify situations where AI should be avoided, delayed, or limited because another source of help is more appropriate.

The key idea

Do not use AI as the main tool when the task requires immediate emergency action, direct observation, accountable professional judgment, guaranteed confidentiality, or a decision that should not be delegated.

Use another source first when:

  • someone may be in immediate danger;
  • the situation requires emergency services or urgent professional care;
  • a qualified person must inspect, examine, test, or certify something;
  • a current official rule, deadline, or instruction controls the outcome;
  • the decision affects another person's rights, employment, safety, or access to services;
  • the information is too sensitive to share safely;
  • you cannot meaningfully review the answer;
  • an incorrect answer could cause serious and irreversible harm.

Four possible decisions

The choice is not always simply “use AI” or “do not use AI.” A safer decision may be:

  1. Use AI normally for low-risk, reviewable assistance.
  2. Use AI with safeguards such as removing identifying details and verifying important claims.
  3. Use another source first when official, current, professional, or emergency guidance is required.
  4. Do not use AI for the task when sharing the information or delegating the judgment is inappropriate.

Example

Situation

A manager wants AI to decide which employee should be laid off.

Why this is a poor use

The decision affects a person's livelihood and may involve legal duties, protected information, incomplete performance context, and organizational accountability.

A limited supporting role

AI might help the manager create a neutral meeting agenda, improve the clarity of a policy summary, or list questions to discuss with human resources. It should not make or rank the final employment decision.

Best practice When stakes rise, reduce AI's authority. Use it for preparation or organization, not final judgment.

Watch for this A person asks AI first because it is convenient, even though the correct first step is calling emergency services, consulting an official source, or contacting a qualified professional.

Try it

Choose the safest first action.

  1. Smoke is coming from an electrical panel.
  2. You want ideas for a weekly meal plan.
  3. You need the current deadline for filing an appeal.
  4. You must decide whether a damaged roof is structurally safe.
  5. You want help organizing questions for a lawyer.

Suggested answers:

  1. Leave danger and contact emergency help; do not troubleshoot with AI.
  2. AI is reasonable with ordinary review.
  3. Check the controlling official source.
  4. Use a qualified inspector or professional.
  5. AI may help prepare questions, while the lawyer provides legal advice.

Apply it

Think of one task where you are tempted to use AI because the answer would be convenient. Decide whether the better choice is normal use, safeguarded use, another source first, or no AI use. State why.

Check yourself

You are ready to continue when you can recognize that the safest first step may be a person, official source, direct inspection, or emergency service rather than an AI conversation.

Pause and decide

When should AI usually not be the final decision-maker?
