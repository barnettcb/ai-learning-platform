How Can AI Help Me Compare Options and Make a Decision?

What you will be able to do

You can use AI to organize alternatives and tradeoffs while preserving your authority over values, priorities, evidence, and the final choice.

The key idea

Ask AI to support the decision process, not to declare a winner before the decision criteria are clear.

A sound comparison has four parts:

  1. define the decision;
  2. identify realistic alternatives;
  3. establish criteria and their relative importance;
  4. compare evidence, uncertainty, and tradeoffs.

The final recommendation should follow from those parts rather than from AI's general preference.

Define the decision precisely

“Which is best?” is usually too vague. State:

  • who the decision is for;
  • the outcome that matters;
  • available options;
  • budget, time, or policy limits;
  • required features;
  • unacceptable outcomes;
  • the information source and date, when relevant.

Separate facts from preferences

Comparison work often mixes three different things:

  • verified facts about each option;
  • assumptions where facts are unavailable;
  • personal priorities that determine how tradeoffs are valued.

Keep those categories visible. A lower price may be a fact; whether price matters more than convenience is a judgment.

Example

Weak request

“Which laptop should I buy?”

Better request

“Compare these three laptops for a college student who mainly writes papers, attends video classes, and travels between home and campus. Budget is $1,000 before tax. Battery life and weight matter more than gaming performance. Use only the specifications I provide, identify missing information, and present the tradeoffs before making a conditional recommendation.”

The better request ties the recommendation to the actual user and available evidence.

Use scoring carefully

A weighted scorecard can clarify priorities, but numbers do not remove judgment. Review:

  • whether the criteria are independent;
  • whether the weights reflect real priorities;
  • whether scores are based on evidence;
  • whether a small score difference is meaningful;
  • whether any non-negotiable requirement should override the total.

Best practice Request a conditional conclusion: “Choose Option A when these priorities dominate; choose Option B when those priorities dominate.” This often exposes tradeoffs better than a single universal answer.

Watch for this The learner asks AI to recommend an option, then treats the fluent explanation as evidence. The criteria, sources, and personal priorities remain hidden.

Try it

Improve this request:

“Tell me which vacation is better.”

One possible revision:

“Compare a three-night city trip and a three-night cabin trip for two adults who want a quiet break. Total budget is $1,500. Driving time, crowd levels, pet access, and indoor backup activities matter. Show verified facts separately from assumptions and explain which option fits under different weather and budget conditions.”

Apply it

Compare at least three genuine alternatives. Write the criteria before requesting a recommendation, then change one important priority and observe whether the conclusion should change.

Check yourself

You are ready to continue when you can explain why the preferred option follows from stated evidence and priorities rather than AI authority.
