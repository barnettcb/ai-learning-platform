How Do I Create a Personal AI Workflow?

What you will be able to do

You can define a practical, repeatable method for deciding, performing, reviewing, and preserving your own AI-assisted work.

The key idea

Build a small workflow around the kinds of tasks you actually do, the risks they carry, and the level of review they deserve.

A personal workflow can use six stages:

  1. Decide: Is AI appropriate, and what role should it play?
  2. Define: What result, audience, context, and constraints matter?
  3. Provide: Which files, facts, examples, or current sources are needed?
  4. Collaborate: Should the task be completed directly, in steps, or through revision?
  5. Review: What must be checked, verified, or approved?
  6. Preserve: What should be saved as a result, template, decision, or handoff?

This is not a fixed prompt formula. It is a decision process that adapts to the task.

Create risk levels

Define simple review levels for yourself. For example:

  • Low: brainstorming or private drafts; quick sense-check;
  • Moderate: work shared with others; factual and completeness review;
  • High: health, money, legal rights, employment, safety, or irreversible action; authoritative evidence and responsible human approval.

The labels may differ, but the review expectation should be clear before the work begins.

Choose default habits

Useful defaults may include:

  • state the desired outcome first;
  • minimize sensitive information;
  • provide source material instead of relying on memory;
  • ask for assumptions when uncertainty matters;
  • verify changing facts;
  • separate generated suggestions from decisions;
  • save final artifacts outside the chat;
  • create a handoff for ongoing projects.

Keep only habits you can realistically maintain.

Example

A manager's workflow uses AI for meeting preparation, document drafting, and project comparison. Each task starts with a defined outcome. Internal data is minimized, current documents are supplied, and anything affecting employees or contracts receives a separate factual and policy review. Reusable templates are saved, while final approvals remain outside the AI conversation.

Best practice Design your workflow from recurring real tasks, then revise it after use. Do not begin with a complicated system you have never tested.

Watch for this A user creates one universal “master prompt” and assumes it replaces judgment, evidence selection, iteration, and review.

Try it

Map the six stages to this task: using AI to prepare for a contractor meeting about an expensive home repair.

Apply it

Write your personal AI workflow on one page. Include common task types, prohibited or restricted uses, default prompting habits, review levels, storage practices, and a rule for escalating to an expert.

Check yourself

You have completed the core curriculum when your workflow explains not only how you ask AI for help, but how you decide, verify, act, and retain responsibility.
