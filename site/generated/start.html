How the Program Works

This program teaches practical competence through four stages:

  1. Understand what AI can and cannot do.
  2. Learn to communicate a task clearly.
  3. Apply the skill to common kinds of work.
  4. Turn successful interactions into reliable workflows.

Each module contains three focused lessons, one applied completion task, and one short readiness check. Follow that sequence before moving to the next module. The site’s Continue learning and Next recommended step panels keep this order for you.

Your first session

  1. Complete the initial self-assessment without looking for “perfect” answers.
  2. Open Module 1 and read its overview.
  3. Finish Lesson 1 and try its action step.
  4. Mark the lesson complete only when you can perform the final check.

A first session usually takes about 20–30 minutes.

Recommended pace

A practical pace is one module per week: three short lessons during the week, followed by the completion task and readiness check. Move faster or slower as needed, but do the activities rather than reading passively.

What counts as completion

A lesson is complete when you can perform its final check, not merely recognize the explanation. A module is complete when you can finish its practical task without a critical safety or reliability error and pass its readiness check.

What you need

  • Basic ability to use a web browser
  • Access to a conversational AI system
  • A willingness to test, review, and revise results

No programming or technical background is required.

Initial Self-Assessment

Choose the response that best describes what you would do today. Your selections stay in this browser. This is diagnostic, not graded.

1. An AI response sounds confident and includes citations.
2. You need help with a confidential workplace problem.
3. Your first response is not useful.
4. You want AI to compare two options.
5. A task involves urgent safety risk.

Repeat this assessment after the capstone and compare your reasoning, not just your selections.

Learning Path

Part I — Understand the Tool

Learn what AI does well, why it can be wrong, when to use it, and what not to share.

Part II — Communicate Clearly

Learn to define the result, provide context, state boundaries, shape the response, and improve weak outputs.

Part III — Apply the Skill

Use distinct workflows for research, writing, files, images, planning, learning, and high-stakes information.

Part IV — Build Reliable Workflows

Turn effective interactions into reviewed, repeatable systems for ongoing work.

Capstone

Complete one meaningful project from decision through verification and documentation.

The first pass is linear. After completion, the platform becomes a searchable reference for individual tasks and concepts.
