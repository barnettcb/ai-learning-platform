# Assessment Framework

## Purpose
Assessment measures practical competence, not recall of terminology.

## Assessment levels

### 1. Recognition
The learner can identify a sound or unsound AI practice in a realistic scenario.

### 2. Repair
The learner can diagnose a weak interaction and make a targeted improvement.

### 3. Construction
The learner can create an effective request or workflow from a task description.

### 4. Judgment
The learner can evaluate an AI result, identify uncertainty, and decide what must be verified or escalated.

### 5. Transfer
The learner can apply the same principle in a different domain without relying on a memorized prompt.

## Module completion standard
A module is complete when the learner can perform its completion task with no critical errors and can briefly explain the reasoning behind the approach.

## Feedback model
Feedback uses three labels:
- **Effective:** The choice is sound and fit for the task.
- **Needs adjustment:** The approach is workable but misses an important element.
- **Unsafe or unreliable:** The approach creates a meaningful privacy, accuracy, or judgment risk.

## Scoring policy
The core course does not use grades or rank learners. Readiness checks report how many scenarios were answered correctly so the learner knows what to review. A check is complete only when every scenario is correct. This is a practice threshold, not a credentialing score.

Applied work is interpreted through three competence states:
- Not yet demonstrated
- Demonstrated with support
- Demonstrated independently

## Capstone rubric
The capstone is evaluated across seven dimensions:
1. Task suitability
2. Problem definition
3. Context and constraints
4. Interaction strategy
5. Evidence and verification
6. Human judgment and accountability
7. Reusability and documentation

A passing capstone has no unsafe or unreliable dimension and demonstrates independent competence in at least five dimensions.
